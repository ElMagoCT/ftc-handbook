What FTC actually is

The competition

  • Teams of up to 15 students, grades 7–12, build one robot that starts inside an 18-inch cube.
  • Matches are 2-vs-2. Your alliance partner in qualification matches is random, so your robot has to score on its own.
  • A new game drops every September at kickoff. Your robot from last year is a starting point, not a finished product.
  • Any COTS part is fair game, plus anything you machine, print, or cut yourself. That freedom is the whole point of FTC.

The other half

  • Judged awards count as much as match wins for advancement. The Inspire Award alone is worth more points than winning the playoffs.
  • Judges look at your engineering process, your outreach, your team plan, and how you carry yourselves. That means Gracious Professionalism all day, not just in the interview room.
  • FIRST's stated goal is getting people into STEM. Teams that actually do that (mentor an FLL team, run a camp, bring in new members) get rewarded for it.

How a season runs

  • Summer
    Pre-season Train new members, run the missions on this site, rebuild the drivetrain, fix the shop. The only time you can try risky ideas (looking at you, differential swerve).
  • Early Sept
    Kickoff Game reveal, Competition Manual, field drawings. Read the manual the same weekend. Start game analysis before touching parts.
  • Sept–Oct
    Prototype and commit Cardboard, PLA, and math. Pick a strategy, then a robot architecture, then CAD it. The SDK for the season ships around kickoff.
  • Mid Oct
    First events open League meets and qualifiers can start about six weeks after kickoff. Some regions run leagues, others run one-day qualifiers.
  • Nov–Feb
    Iterate Compete, watch the video, fix the two things that cost the most points, repeat. Portfolio gets updated after every event, not the night before.
  • Dec–Mar
    League tournaments and regionals Judging, alliance selection, and advancement points. Regional championships run through late March.
  • Late April
    FIRST Championship Houston. A small slice of teams advance. Everyone else starts the post-season debrief and the recruiting push.

Exact dates for the current season are on the Season page.

The house rules

Seven ideas that show up on every page of this site. They come from a lot of broken robots and a few great ones.

01

Keep it simple

  • Functionality over coolness.
  • If a part needs five custom brackets to mount, the design is wrong, not the brackets.
02

Touch it, own it

  • An intake should have full control of the game element the moment it makes contact.
  • Every second lining up is a second not scoring.
03

Design for the builders

  • If you cannot fit a finger where a screw goes, redesign it.
  • Nobody remembers this and whole days get lost to it.
04

Think of your coders

  • A fully articulated arm sounds neat.
  • Code takes time and experience.
  • Put the Driver Hub port somewhere a cable can reach.
05

Strategy first

  • Decide what the robot needs to do before deciding how.
  • A simpler mechanism that fits the strategy beats a capable one that does not.
06

Innovate on purpose

  • Simple is how you finish. Creative is how you win.
  • One bold mechanism per robot, prototyped early, and the rest boring and reliable.
07

Write it down

  • Every test, every failure, every number.
  • The portfolio writes itself if the notebook is honest.
  • Judges can tell when it was made up in April.

Using this site

Find things

  • Press / or the search button to search every page and the glossary.
  • Words with a dotted underline open a short definition. Tap one to try it.
