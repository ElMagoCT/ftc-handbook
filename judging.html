Judging & Portfolio

Judges can only score what you show them.

Judging changed a lot in 2025 and again in 2026: points-based advancement, Motivate split into Reach and Sustain, no Control Award form, no Engineering Notebook requirement, and a renamed interview. This page is the current system as of the 2026–27 Competition Manual, and how to prepare for it starting in September, not April.

2026–27 rulesManual §4 and §6Checked Sept 2026

How judging works

The day

  1. Portfolio goes in. One printed copy, handed over at your Initial Interview unless the event says otherwise (A202). Bring a second for the pit.
  2. Initial Interview. At least 10 minutes with a panel of two or three judges (A205). About five minutes of uninterrupted presentation, then open questions (A207). Two question-bank questions are asked of every team at the event, one about the machine and one about team attributes.
  3. Pit interviews. Judges walk the pits and follow up. Informal, unscheduled, and where a lot of tiebreaks get decided.
  4. Deliberations. Judges compare notes against the award criteria. They may only use what they saw and heard at this event, this season. Match results, rankings, and social media are off limits (§6.1.1).
  5. Feedback. Every team gets a written feedback form (A212). Read it on the bus and fix the top item before the next event.

The rules that trip teams up

  • A203: no interview, no awards. It is mandatory.
  • A204: at least two students. The robot is encouraged but optional; you can win awards without it and regardless of inspection status.
  • A208: one adult may sit in silently. No coaching from the back.
  • A210: no photos, audio, or video in the interview.
  • A215: a team can win or be runner-up for only one team-judged award per event. Inspire is the exception in that it is awarded first.
  • A213: Inspire is only available in your home region (except at Championship).
  • Interview formats vary by event in 2026–27: scheduled in-person, unscheduled at the pit, or remote video (§6.1.2). Ask which yours is.
Tip
  • Judges read dozens of portfolios in a morning.
  • They remember the team that answered the question they asked, showed one clear number per claim, and had every member say something.
  • They do not remember the longest presentation.

The awards

  • Table 6-1 in the manual.
  • Three groups: Inspire and Think stand alone, three Team Attribute awards, three Machine, Creativity & Innovation awards.
  • Plus Judges' Choice.
  • Motivate and Promote no longer exist.
Top award · portfolio required

Inspire Award

  • For the team that is a strong contender in every other category and shows FIRST values.
  • Worth 60 advancement points to the winner, 30 and 15 to runners-up.
  • Inspire winners almost always advance.
  • Must be competitive for Think, a Team Attribute award, and a Machine award at the same time.
  • Balance is the whole game: a great robot with no outreach does not win it, and neither does the reverse.
  • Gracious Professionalism observed all day counts.
Documentation · portfolio required

Think Award

  • For the engineering process, as documented.
  • The portfolio must show at least one of: evidence of an engineering process, lessons learned, comparing design choices, math-based decisions.
  • Show iterations: what failed, what you measured, what you changed.
  • One real calculation (gear ratio for arm torque, cycle time math) beats ten adjectives.
  • Cite the notebook or data even though the notebook is not collected.

Team Attribute awards portfolio not required

Connect Award

  • Partnerships with the STEM and engineering community, plus a plan to develop the team's skills.
  • Mentors from industry, shop access from a sponsor, visits to engineers, and what you learned from each.

Reach Award new 2025

  • Bringing new people into FIRST.
  • Recruiting, starting teams, demos that led to sign-ups, mentoring FLL.
  • Judges want who, how many, and what changed for them.

Sustain Award new 2025

  • Organizational, financial, and leadership sustainability.
  • A budget, a fundraising plan, leadership transitions, how the team survives graduation.

Machine, Creativity & Innovation awards

Innovate Award sponsored by RTX

  • A creative, working robot design or mechanism solving a game challenge in a new way.
  • The design must actually function; a cool idea that does not work is a Think story, not an Innovate one.

Control Award portfolio required

  • Sensors and software that make the robot more effective.
  • The portfolio must summarize each control component, the challenge it solves, and how it works.
  • No separate submission form anymore.
  • Full code is not needed; a diagram and a result are.

Design Award

  • Industrial design: functional, well built, and looks intentional, with a design process behind it.
  • Clean wiring, thoughtful packaging, a robot that is obviously finished.

Judges' Choice

Optional and discretionary, but since 2025 it earns advancement points like the other non-Inspire awards (12/6/3).

Compass Award

  • For a mentor, nominated by students with a 40–60 second video.
  • Optional, offered at regional championships and the FIRST Championship.

FIRST Leadership Award

  • Replaced Dean's List.
  • Two 10th or 11th graders nominated per team.
  • Nominations open in September and close mid-December.
  • Start the essays early.

What changed, and when history
  • 2024–25
    INTO THE DEEP. Single Competition Manual replaces Game Manual Parts 1 and 2. Ordered advancement list, Motivate and Promote still exist, Control Award has a separate form.
  • 2025–26
    DECODE. Points-based advancement. Motivate retired, split into Reach and Sustain. Judges' Choice earns points. "Presentation interview" renamed Structured Interview. Portfolio content limited to the current calendar year. Dean's List becomes the FIRST Leadership Award.
  • 2026–27
    BIOBUZZ. Structured Interview renamed Initial Interview, with three formats an event can choose. Award lineup unchanged. No Control Award form. Servo limit cut to 8.

Advancement points

Since 2025 there is no ordered list. Every team earns points at each event and the top totals in a region advance. Table 4-1 in the manual.

What it means for strategy

  • Inspire is worth more than winning the event. A balanced team with a mid-field robot can out-point a great robot that skips judging.
  • A Think or Connect win (12) plus a finalist run (20) plus a top-8 rank beats a playoff win alone.
  • Getting picked early matters: first pick on alliance one is 20 points.
  • Tiebreakers run total → judged points → playoff → alliance → qualification, then match points.
  • Your first three events count for advancement. Choose them so judging and matches both have time to mature.

The Engineering Portfolio

The rules (A201–A202)

Length
One cover page plus up to 15 content pages. Page 16 does not get read.
Size
US Letter or A4. If submitted digitally, under 15 MB.
Cover
Team number required. Name, logo, sponsors, table of contents optional. Not judged.
Content window
Work from January 1 of the kickoff year onward. Earlier seasons only as a "how we grew" reference.
Text
Avoid fonts under 10 pt and low contrast. Judges are told not to squint.
Links
Judges do not follow links or watch videos, and will not take extra papers. Everything must be on the page.
Privacy
First name and last initial. No emails or phone numbers.
AI tools
Allowed, with a credit footnote saying where you used them.
Submission
One printed copy at the Initial Interview by default. Keep a spare in the pit.

A 15-page layout that covers every award

PagesContentFeeds
1Team overview: who, structure, goals, season in one pictureInspire
2Game analysis and strategy: the scoring table, the decisionThink
3–7Robot: one page per subsystem. Requirement, options compared, math, iterations with photos, resultThink, Innovate, Design
8Control: sensors, software structure, auto, one data graphControl
9Connect: mentors, sponsors, engineers, and what each taught you; skills planConnect
10Reach: recruiting, demos, teams started, with countsReach
11Sustain: budget, funding, leadership pipelineSustain
12Lessons learned and next stepsThink, Inspire
13–15Flex: more subsystem depth, testing data, competition iterationsWhatever is weakest

Design pages that work

  • Requirement → options considered → why this one → math → v1 photo → what broke → v2 photo → result.
  • Photos and CAD with callouts, not paragraphs.
  • A number per claim: "cycle time 6.1 s → 4.3 s after roller redesign."

Outreach pages that work

  • Who, how many, when, what happened after. "Demoed to 200 students at the STEM night; 6 joined the FLL team we mentor."
  • Sponsor logo plus what they actually gave and what you did with it.
  • Photos with captions. Judges may verify claims; never round up.

Formatting

  • Team number in every footer. Consistent template, 11 pt body, wide margins, white background.
  • Section headers named after the awards so judges find them in ten seconds.
  • Proofread by someone outside the team.
Notebook: not required, still worth keeping detail
  • The manual no longer mentions an Engineering Notebook. Nothing is collected except the portfolio.
  • Keep one anyway. Every meeting: date, who was there, what was decided, what was tested, numbers. It is the raw material for every portfolio page and every interview answer.
  • Digital is fine (a shared doc or Notion). Photos every meeting. Judges may ask to see it in the pit, and a real one impresses.
  • Portfolio pages get written from the notebook after each event, not the night before the next one.

The Initial Interview

The five minutes

  • Open with who you are and the one sentence that sums up the season. Then hit the awards you are actually competing for: robot, control, one outreach story, sustainability.
  • Every student speaks. Assign sections by who did the work; judges can tell.
  • Bring the robot if you can (optional, encouraged) and demo one thing that works. A 15-second live claw grab is worth a page of slides.
  • Practice it ten times with a timer. Then practice being interrupted, because you will be.
  • Hand the portfolio over at the start so judges can follow along.

The questions

  • Two questions from FIRST's question bank are asked of every team: one about the machine, one about team attributes. Read the question bank and rehearse all of them.
  • Then judges dig into your portfolio. If a page says "we chose 312 rpm motors," someone will ask why.
  • Answer the question asked, with a number, in under a minute. Then stop. Silence is fine.
  • If you do not know, say who on the team does and let them answer. Never make it up.
  • Gracious Professionalism is being judged in the room too: how you treat each other when someone stumbles.
Question types to expect practice list

Machine

  • Walk us through how you chose your drivetrain.
  • What was the biggest design failure this season and what did you learn?
  • How do you know your intake is reliable? What did you measure?
  • What sensors do you use and what problem does each solve?
  • What would you change with two more weeks?

Team attributes

  • How does your team make decisions when people disagree?
  • Tell us about a partnership with an engineer or company and what you learned from it.
  • How did you bring new people into FIRST this year?
  • What happens to the team when your seniors graduate?
  • Describe a time you showed Gracious Professionalism at an event.

Watch Mr. Mainieri's Gracious Professionalism playlist for the culture side. Loom recordings of our own judging workshops are linked on the Resources page.

Pit judging

Always staffed

  • One or two members in the pit all day who can answer any question about the robot and the season.
  • Rotate so drive team is not stuck there.

Something to look at

  • Second portfolio on the table, a photo board or tablet slideshow of outreach, a spare printed part or the v1 mechanism that failed.
  • Judges love the failed one.

Look like a team

  • Shirts, a banner, a clean pit.
  • Be friendly to visitors and to the team next to you; judges notice pits from a distance before they walk up.

Outreach that counts

Three kinds. Each feeds a different award. Track every one with a number and a photo.

Feeds Reach

Community

  • Robot demos at libraries, fairs, school STEM nights. Let kids drive in a slow mode.
  • Summer camps and workshops. Share the curriculum afterward.
  • Mentoring FLL teams, starting new FTC teams.
  • Volunteering at FLL and FTC events as referees, scorekeepers, field reset.
Feeds Connect

Engineering and STEM community

  • Visits to engineering firms and machine shops, presenting the robot and getting design feedback.
  • Recruiting mentors from industry and universities.
  • Learning a skill from a professional (CAM, welding, PCB design) and using it on the robot.
  • Sharing CAD, code, or portfolios with other teams.
Feeds Sustain

Organizational

  • Sponsor pitches with a real budget and a follow-up report to the sponsor.
  • Grants, school funding, fundraising events.
  • Leadership transition plan: who runs each subsystem next year and how they are being trained.
  • Recruiting pipeline from feeder schools and FLL.

Rules of thumb

  • Impact over count. Ten demos nobody remembers lose to one camp that produced a new team.
  • Never exaggerate. Judges have heard "500 people" a hundred times and will ask how you counted.
  • Invite other FTC teams to your events. It is Coopertition and it doubles the impact.
  • Close the loop: what happened because of the event. That sentence is what wins Reach.

A documentation plan for the season

  • Sept
    Set up the notebook and the outreach log. Assign a documentation lead (this is a real job, not a punishment). Photograph kickoff game analysis.
  • Oct
    Portfolio template built with the 15-page layout and team branding. Pages 1 and 2 drafted. Leadership Award nominees chosen; essays started.
  • Before event 1
    Portfolio v1. Robot pages with v1 mechanisms and honest test data. Interview rehearsed five times. Question bank reviewed.
  • After each event
    Read the feedback form. Update the pages it criticizes. Add competition iterations with before-and-after numbers.
  • Dec 15
    Leadership Award nominations due.
  • Before regionals
    Portfolio v3. Every award section reviewed against the manual's Required and Encouraged criteria. Outside proofread. Two copies printed.

Reading the criteria

Since 2025 each award lists numbered Required and Encouraged criteria (Tables 6-2 to 6-9) instead of the old Beginning / Developing / Accomplished / Exemplary ladder. You still see that ladder on older summary sheets; treat it as history.

How to use them

  • Copy the criteria for each award you are targeting into a checklist.
  • For every Required item, point to the exact portfolio page and the exact interview sentence that covers it.
  • Encouraged items are the tiebreakers. Cover as many as are honest.
  • If you cannot point to a page for a Required item, you are not eligible for that award. Fix the page or change the target.

Missions

Mission J1

One subsystem page

  • Write one portfolio page for a mechanism you worked on: requirement, options, math, iterations, result.
  • Photos with callouts, one number per claim, under 10 pt nowhere.
Done when a mentor can identify the Think criteria it satisfies without you telling them.
Mission J2

Mock interview

  • Five-minute presentation with the whole team, then 10 minutes of questions from two adults using the question bank.
  • Film it. Watch it. Fix the three worst moments.
Done when every member spoke, every answer had a number, and nobody said "um, I think Alex knows."
Mission J3

Outreach with a loop closed

  • Plan and run one outreach event.
  • Track attendance with a sign-in sheet, photograph it, and follow up two weeks later to record what changed.
Done when the outreach log has a row with a real number in every column, including Outcome.
Mission J4

Criteria audit

  • Take the current portfolio and the manual's award tables.
  • For each award you want, map every Required criterion to a page and every Encouraged one to a page or "missing."
  • Present the gaps to the team with a fix for each.
Done when no Required criterion for a targeted award says "missing."
