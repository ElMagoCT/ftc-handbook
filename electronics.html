Electronics

One battery, one brain, eight motors, eight servos.

The FTC control system is small and fixed, which is good news: there is a right way to wire it and you can learn the whole thing in an afternoon. This page covers what plugs into what, what each motor and servo is good for, the sensors worth owning, and the wiring rules inspectors check.

The control system

Robot

REV Control Hub (REV-31-1595)

  • An Android computer plus motor, servo, and sensor ports.
  • Runs your code, hosts its own Wi-Fi, talks to the Driver Station.
  • The only officially supported Robot Controller (R701).
Robot

REV Expansion Hub (REV-31-1153)

  • The same ports without the computer.
  • Connects to the Control Hub over RS485.
  • You may add at most one (R701).
  • Needed past four motors.
Driver

REV Driver Hub (REV-31-1596)

  • Handheld Android device running the Driver Station app.
  • Gamepads plug in by USB.
  • Any Android device is allowed, but the Driver Hub is the only supported one (R901).
Driver

Gamepads

Wired only (R904). Logitech F310, Xbox, PS4 and PS5 controllers all work. Two per team, plus a spare.

Startup, Wi-Fi, and the configuration file detail
  • The Control Hub broadcasts its own Wi-Fi network named after your team (R705: ####-RC). Change the default password (R711). The Driver Hub joins it.
  • The robot configuration on the Driver Station maps each port to a name ("leftFront", "armMotor"). Your code looks devices up by those names. Save it with a name that matches the robot revision.
  • Robot Controller app and Driver Station app must be the same major.minor version. Update both together with the REV Hardware Client.
  • Keep the Control Hub's Wi-Fi band on 5 GHz at events if the venue supports it; 2.4 GHz gets crowded.
  • Blinking status LED colors mean things. Green is happy. Blue blinks with the address. Solid red is a problem; look up the pattern in REV's docs.

Motors

Eight DC motors maximum (R503), from the approved list (R501, Table 12-1). Every one of them is a brushed 12 V motor with a gearbox and an encoder; the differences are ratio, form factor, and mounting.

Most common

goBILDA Yellow Jacket (5203 / 5204)

  • One motor, ten gearbox ratios.
  • 5 mm hex output on 5203 (8 mm REX on 5204), built-in encoder, mounts on the goBILDA pattern.
  • Stall current 9.2 A.
  • 312 rpm (19.2:1): mecanum drivetrain on 96–104 mm wheels.
  • 435 rpm (13.7:1): faster drivetrain, intake rollers, slide spools.
  • 117 or 84 rpm: arm pivots with an extra external stage.
  • 1150 or 1620 rpm: flywheels, fast intakes.
REV

HD Hex + UltraPlanetary

  • 6000 rpm bare motor with snap-together 3:1, 4:1, and 5:1 cartridges.
  • Build 12:1 (500 rpm) to 60:1 (100 rpm).
  • 5 mm hex output, 8.5 A stall.
  • Cheaper and lighter than a Yellow Jacket at the same ratio.
  • Cartridges wear under drivetrain shock loads; carry spares.
  • Nominal ratios are approximate (a "20:1" is 18.9:1). Use the real number for encoder math.
REV

Core Hex

125 rpm, 72:1, right-angle female 5 mm hex. Small, cheap, weak (4.4 A stall).

  • Light mechanisms, indexers, low-speed rollers.
  • Too slow and weak for a drivetrain.
  • Encoder is 288 counts per output revolution.

Rules and gotchas detail
  • R501: motors must be on the approved list. Yellow Jacket 5000/520x, REV HD Hex and Core Hex, AndyMark NeveRest, Studica Maverick, TETRIX MAX and TorqueNADO, and a few others. No random Amazon motors.
  • R504: you may swap gearboxes; you may not modify the motor itself.
  • Two motors on one port is allowed. They will run the same direction and share the encoder port. Fine for two intake rollers, never for a drivetrain.
  • A stalled motor pulls about 9 A. Two stalled motors and the battery sags; four and the Control Hub reboots. Never let the drivetrain push a wall.
  • Motor wires are 18 AWG minimum unless the vendor's attached leads are thinner (R609). Do not extend them with thin servo wire.
  • Encoder cables carry a tiny signal. Route them away from motor power leads and strain-relieve both ends; the encoder side is the one that comes unplugged mid-match.

Servos

Eight servos maximum (R503), each under 8 W mechanical output and 4 A stall at 6 V (R502). Bring the spec sheet to inspection for anything not on the pre-approved list.

$37

goBILDA Dual Mode (Torque / Speed / Super Speed)

  • 300° travel, or continuous rotation via the programmer.
  • Torque: 21.6 kg·cm at 0.20 s/60°.
  • Speed: 9.3 kg·cm at 0.09 s.
  • Super Speed: 4.7 kg·cm at 230 rpm equivalent.
  • The default FTC servo.
  • The 5-turn version (1800°) is the same servo geared for winches and long-travel wrists.
$100

Axon MAX / MINI (MK2)

  • MAX: 34 kg·cm at 0.12 s/60°, 78 g, brushless, steel gears, analog position feedback wire, 350°.
  • MINI: 25 kg·cm at 0.09 s, 67 g, low profile.
  • Fast and strong inside the rule.
  • The feedback wire (analog port) lets code know where the claw actually is.
  • Worth it on the claw and wrist; overkill on a flap.
$30

REV Smart Robot Servo

13.5 kg·cm, 270° or continuous with the SRS programmer. Reliable, moderate torque, the REV-ecosystem default.

Power

REV Servo Power Module (REV-11-1144)

  • Six channels with their own 6 V, 15 A supply from the battery.
  • Use it when high-torque servos twitch or reset because the hub's 6 V rail sags.
  • Legal power device (R505).
Power

REV Servo Hub (REV-11-1855)

  • Six channels, configurable 5–7.4 V (6 V default), 15 A total, connected over RS485 or USB.
  • Legal (R505).
  • The SDK exposes basic servo control; you still count these toward the eight.
Also legal

goBILDA Servo Power Injector, Studica Servo Power Block

Same idea, other vendors. All in Table 12-3. Anything not in that table is not a legal servo power source.

Servo tips detail
  • Program travel limits with the servo programmer before mounting. A servo driving into a hard stop at full torque strips its own gears.
  • Continuous-rotation mode turns a servo into a tiny motor that does not count against the motor limit. Good for indexers and light rollers.
  • Two servos on one port with a Y-cable is legal and useful for a two-servo claw. They must agree; mirror one in code.
  • Servo extension cables lose voltage. Keep runs short and use 22 AWG (the rule minimum for servo leads).
  • A servo holding position under load draws current the whole time. Design so the mechanism rests against a stop, not against the servo.
  • The ServoImplEx class in the SDK lets you set PWM range and disable a servo to save power when idle.

I²C, digital, analog: which port for what detail
  • I²C is a shared two-wire bus. Each hub has four. Two sensors with the same address (color V3 and 2 m distance are both 0x52) cannot share a bus. Spread sensors across buses; every I²C read costs loop time.
  • Digital ports read on/off. Each JST port carries two channels (0 and 1, 2 and 3). REV touch sensors use the second channel (n+1) of the port they are plugged into; this trips up everyone once.
  • Analog ports read 0–3.3 V. Two channels per port, same idea.
  • Encoder ports are quadrature inputs paired with each motor port, but you can plug a dead-wheel pod or through-bore encoder into an encoder port whose motor slot is empty or whose motor does not need one.
  • Sensor cables are 28 AWG signal wire (R609). Buy them premade; do not crimp JST PH by hand.

Power

Where the battery's energy actually goes is on the Design page. Short version: heat in stalled motors, then friction.

Battery and main power

  • R601: exactly one approved 12 V NiMH battery (REV, goBILDA, AndyMark, TETRIX, Studica, Matrix, WATTOS). No LiPo, no LiFePO4, no second main battery.
  • The battery's built-in fuse may be replaced only with a COTS 20 A ATM mini blade fuse. You may swap connectors to XT30, Anderson Powerpole, or equivalent.
  • R603: exactly one approved main power switch, reachable without touching a mechanism, away from pinch points. Label it.
  • R602: a COTS USB power bank under 100 Wh may power only isolated things: a powered USB hub, a phone Robot Controller, a self-contained camera.
  • R605: the frame is not a current path. Grounding is only through an approved resistive strap.
Battery care detail
  • A full NiMH pack reads about 13.5–14 V off the charger and sags toward 12 V under load. Swap below 12.5 V at rest; matches get slow and servos get flaky below that.
  • Use the vendor's smart charger. Do not leave packs on a dumb charger overnight.
  • Number every battery and rotate. Retire any pack that is swollen, gets hot charging, or drops voltage fast.
  • Four batteries and a charger is the minimum for a competition day.
  • Strap the battery down so it cannot shift. A battery that slides into a mechanism ends a match.

Wiring rules and practice

The rules (R609–R613)

18 AWG
Main 12 V battery wiring and motor power (circuits carrying 11–20 A)
22 AWG
Servo and PWM leads, LEDs, small motors (Core Hex), anything ≤10 A
28 AWG
Signal only: I²C, digital, analog, encoder, RS485
Colors
Positive is red, yellow, white, brown, or black with a stripe. Ground is black or blue.
Custom circuits
Allowed, but they may not output more than 5 V regulated (except LEDs) and may not sit in the battery → switch → hub → actuator power path. No buck or boost converters on servo power.
Connectors
Vendor-attached wires are exempt from gauge rules. Do not parallel small wires to fake a bigger one.

Good practice

  • Strain relief everything. A zip tie or cable anchor within 50 mm (2 in) of every connector. Most match disconnects are a JST pulled loose by a moving wire.
  • Service loops on anything that moves. Move the mechanism by hand through full travel and watch every wire.
  • Label both ends of every cable with the port name from the configuration. Tape and a fine marker.
  • Route signal wires away from motor power where you can. Twisted encoder cables pick up less noise.
  • Hubs where you can see the LEDs and reach the USB port (R606 and your own sanity).
  • XT30s click. If one does not click, the pins are worn; replace it before the event.
  • Keep a printed wiring diagram in the pit binder. Judges and inspectors both like it, and you will need it at 7:45 AM.

Inspection checklist (electrical)

  • One approved battery, one approved switch, fuse intact
  • Motor and servo count within 8 and 8; all motors on the approved list
  • Servo spec sheets for anything not pre-approved
  • Wire gauges and colors per R609–R610
  • No frame grounding except an approved strap
  • Control Hub Wi-Fi renamed and password changed; Bluetooth off
  • Robot Controller and Driver Station app versions match and are current
  • Hubs, fuse, and wiring visible on request

Field kit

  • Spare XT30 pigtails, JST PH sensor cables in every length, servo extensions
  • Spare Yellow Jacket in your drivetrain ratio, spare servo of each type
  • Multimeter, wire strippers, crimper if you use Powerpoles, heat shrink, lighter
  • USB-C cable that works and a spare
  • A spare Control Hub if the budget allows; nothing else on the robot is a single point of failure the same way

Static discharge (ESD)

What happens

  • Wheels on foam tiles build static on the frame. When a wire or the hub gets close enough, it discharges through the electronics.
  • Symptoms: the robot disconnects for a few seconds mid-match, a sensor stops responding, or the hub reboots when you touch it.
  • Dry venues in winter are the worst.

What to do

  • Follow FIRST's ESD mitigation guide: an approved resistive grounding strap from the hub's ground to the frame (R605), so charge bleeds off slowly instead of arcing.
  • Mount hubs on plastic or with standoffs, not bare metal to metal, and keep wires from rubbing the frame.
  • Anti-static spray on the wheels helps a little. A fabric-softener sheet wiped on the tiles helps at practice, not at events.
  • Keep every plug clicked and strain-relieved so a brief glitch does not become a lost cable.

Missions

Mission E1

Wire a hub from scratch

  • Battery, switch, Control Hub, one motor, one servo, one touch sensor.
  • Name them in the configuration, then read all three from a test OpMode on the Driver Hub.
Done when the motor spins, the servo moves, and the sensor shows on telemetry, and every wire has strain relief.
Mission E2

Servo legality check

  • Pick three servos you own or want. Find stall torque and speed at 6 V on the spec sheets.
  • Compute mechanical power with the rule's formula.
  • Decide which are legal and which need a note for inspection.
Done when you have a one-page servo sheet you could hand an inspector.
Mission E3

Wiring diagram

  • Draw the full robot wiring: every hub port, device name, wire gauge, and fuse.
  • Check it against R601–R613.
Done when a new member can rewire a hub from the diagram alone, and it goes in the pit binder.
Mission E4

Brownout hunt

  • Log battery voltage from the hub while driving, stalling the drive against a wall, and running the lift.
  • Plot it. Find the moment servos misbehave.
  • Propose the fix (gearing, a Servo Power Module, driver training) with the data.
Done when you have a voltage graph with the events labeled and one change that removes the dip.
